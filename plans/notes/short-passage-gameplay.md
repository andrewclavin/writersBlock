# Short Passage Gameplay

**Origin**: Emerged from repeated playtesting of the Tale of Two Cities opening paragraph. Replaying a short, self-contained passage felt surprisingly engaging — the word bank is tractable, phrases cascade satisfyingly, and the rhythm of discovery-through-repetition ("it was the ___ of ___") clicks faster than expected.

## Observation

A full novel's word bank is enormous — thousands of unique tokens, phrase families that span chapters, cascades that may never fire because the lockstep alignment is vanishingly rare across 80,000 words. A paragraph or half-page excerpt inverts all of that:

- **The bank is graspable.** Players can scan the full lexicon in seconds and spot merge opportunities without scrolling.
- **Phrases repeat.** Structural repetition (anaphora, parallel clauses) means one merge can cascade across many slots, delivering the core dopamine hit quickly.
- **Sessions are completable.** A 60–120 word passage can be finished in a few minutes, making it viable for daily-puzzle or coffee-break play.
- **Replays teach.** Because the passage is short enough to memorize, replaying shifts the goal from "figure out the text" to "optimize phrase chains and cascade order" — a speedrun-like layer.

## Potential Formats

- **Daily passage**: Curated excerpt (famous opening line, poem stanza, speech fragment). One per day, shared leaderboard on completion time or cascade count.
- **Chapter bites**: Break a novel into paragraph-sized chunks. Players unlock the next chunk by completing the current one — the novel unfolds through play.
- **Thematic packs**: "Gothic openings," "Hemingway sentences," "Shakespeare soliloquies." Short passages grouped by voice or style.
- **Practice mode**: Replay any completed passage to improve phrase-chain strategy. Track personal best cascades.

## Open Questions

- Does the cascade mechanic scale well to 500+ word passages, or does the lockstep algorithm need a different strategy at that size?
- Should short-passage mode use a simplified lexicon (no merge, just place) for onboarding, then graduate to the full merge-and-cascade loop?
- How does scoring work? Completion time, number of cascades triggered, fewest manual placements, or some composite?
